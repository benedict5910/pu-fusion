## Check 1 – Motor torque, current and speed stability at both operating conditions

### 1.1 Operating conditions
| | Speed | Radial load on test bearing |
|---|---|---|
| C1 / C2 | 900 rpm | 20 N / 50 N |
| C3 / C4 | 1,500 rpm | 20 N / 50 N |

### 1.2 Load torque (Palmgren/SKF simplified friction model, 608-2Z)
- **Inputs:**
  - 608: dm = 15 mm, C0 = 1.37 kN.
  - Grease base-oil viscosity ≈ 100 mm²/s.
  - f0 = 2 nominal; 4 worst case (cold or new grease).
  - 2Z shields are non-contact, so there is no seal torque. This is why the supports use 608-2Z in machined housings instead of sealed KP08 inserts.
- **Bearing loads (overhung layout, L = 120 mm, a = 30 mm):**
  - Support B carries 1.25 F.
  - Support A carries 0.25 F.

| | Friction, nominal | Friction, worst |
|---|---|---|
| 900 rpm | 4.2–4.4 mN·m | 8.2–8.5 mN·m |
| 1,500 rpm | 5.8–6.0 mN·m | 11.5–11.8 mN·m |

- **Speed-dependent grease churning dominates.** Going from 20 N to 50 N adds only ≈ 0.25 mN·m.
- **Start-up:** accelerating 0 → 1,500 rpm in 2 s (J ≈ 6.1×10⁻⁵ kg·m²) adds 4.8 mN·m transiently.

### 1.3 Motor operating points
- **Motor:** 775-class 12 V brushed DC, **≈ 4,500 rpm no-load winding**, Ø5 mm round shaft.
  - Example: Zbotic AI6847, ₹471, in stock.
  - The seller gives no motor constants, so the check uses the **RS-775 datasheet row for the 12 V / 4,550 rpm winding (770-4087)** as the representative model:
    - R = 0.85 Ω;
    - Kt = ke = 24.7 mN·m/A;
    - I0 = 0.6 A at 12 V;
    - nominal point 3,500 rpm / 2.8 A.
  - The purchased motor's own R, Kt and I0 are measured in stage S4 and the check is re-run with them (gate M1).

| Condition | Torque | Current | Voltage | Electrical power |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 900 rpm, nominal | 4.2–4.4 mN·m | 0.53 A | 2.8 V | 1.5 W |
| 900 rpm, worst + accel | 13.3 mN·m | 0.89 A | 3.1 V | 2.8 W |
| 1,500 rpm, nominal | 5.8–6.0 mN·m | 0.64 A | 4.4 V | 2.9 W |
| 1,500 rpm, worst + accel | 16.6 mN·m | 1.07 A | 4.8 V | 5.1 W |

- **Torque margin:**
  - Worst running + accelerating load is 16.6 mN·m.
  - The representative winding delivers 54 mN·m at its nominal point: **3.3× margin**.
- **Start-up:**
  - With the supply **current-limited at 2.0 A**, the available start torque is 42 mN·m, more than 3× the worst friction. Start is reliable, and the start current is bounded by the supply, not the motor.
- **Current:**
  - ≤ 1.1 A worst case, inside the ACS712 ±5 A range and the 2 A CC limit.
  - Copper loss ≤ 1 W, so heating is negligible against a 100 W-class motor.

### 1.4 Speed stability – voltage control alone is NOT sufficient
- **Open-loop sensitivity:** 13.3 rpm per mN·m of load torque.
- **Predicted warm-up drift at fixed voltage (≈ +3.8 % in total):**

| Cause | 900 rpm | 1,500 rpm |
|---|---|---|
| Grease warm-up (friction −30 %) | +17 rpm | +24 rpm |
| Ferrite magnet −0.2 %/K over ≈ 15 K | +27 rpm | +45 rpm |
| Copper resistance | −10 rpm | −12 rpm |
| Supply regulation (linear CV) | ≈ 1 rpm | ≈ 1 rpm |
| Radial load 20 → 50 N | < 0.1 rpm | < 0.1 rpm |

- **Decision: speed is regulated by procedure, not assumed.**
  1. **Thermal warm-up:** run ≥ 30 min at the condition before the first recording. Rpm is logged continuously and the run is accepted as warm when the 5-min rpm slope is < 0.2 %/min.
  2. **Trim-and-hold:** before each 60 s recording, trim the supply voltage to the target rpm (10 mV step ≈ 3.9 rpm, i.e. 0.43 % at 900 rpm), then **do not touch the supply during the recording**. Automatic trimming with a programmable supply is a later option only, not needed for rev A.
  3. **Per-recording acceptance:** mean rpm within ±1 % of target, and rpm standard deviation over the recording ≤ 0.3 %. Recordings outside these limits are rejected and repeated, never kept and corrected afterwards.
  4. **Speed handling in analysis:** every window carries measured rpm from tach edge timestamps. Fault orders use measured speed, and features are already speed-normalised.
- **Risk:** at 900 rpm the motor runs at ≈ 2.8 V, where brush-contact drop is a larger share of the voltage.
  - Gate M3 (below) tests this directly.
  - **If 900 rpm fails gate M3, the low-speed condition moves to 1,200 rpm** (recorded protocol change before any data). The remaining fallback is a lower-kV 775 winding, not the gearmotor.

### 1.5 Motor acceptance gates (stage S4, before any experiment)
| Gate | Pass condition |
|---|---|
| M1 Constants | Measured R ≤ 1.5 Ω (locked rotor at ≤ 1 A under CC); ke ≥ 18 mN·m/A (back-EMF vs rpm); I0 at 1,500 rpm ≤ 0.8 A. Re-run `rig_design_calcs.py` with the measured values: torque margin ≥ 2.5× |
| M2 Start | Starts 5 out of 5 times at each condition with CC 2.0 A; supply returns to CV within 1 s |
| M3 Speed | After warm-up and trim, 10 consecutive 60 s recordings at each condition all meet ±1 % mean / ≤ 0.3 % std |
| M4 Thermal | Motor case temperature rise ≤ 40 K after 60 min at 1,500 rpm / 50 N |
| M5 Tach | Hall rpm within 1 % of an optical tachometer at both speeds |

## Check 2 – Mechanical design (drawing `docs/drawings/rig_revA.pdf`)

### 2.1 Layout
- **Arrangement, along x from the motor plate:**
  - 775 motor bolted to a machined face plate (pilot bore on the motor boss);
  - jaw coupling 5×8;
  - Support A (locating, x = 47);
  - unbalance disc (mid-span, x = 107);
  - Support B (floating, x = 167);
  - **overhung test bearing** (x = 197, a = 30 mm) in a floating housing.
- **Shaft:** Ø8 h6 hardened, 195 mm long, centre height 40.00 mm.
- **Why overhung:** a test-bearing change needs no shaft disassembly. Release the springs, remove the end collar, slide off the test cartridge (housing + 608 + covers).

### 2.2 Load transfer and strength
- **Load path:** spring force → test housing → outer ring → balls → inner ring → shaft → supports A/B → base. The spring reaction goes through the M5 studs into the same base, so the whole loop closes inside the base.

| Check | Result | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Support loads | R_B = 1.25 F, R_A = 0.25 F | Max 62.5 N vs 608 C0 = 1,370 N |
| L10 life | > 10⁶ h | No wear drift during the experiments |
| Shaft bending at B | 1.5 N·m → 30 MPa (60 MPa with notch factor 2 at a collar) | Hardened shaft endurance ≫ 200 MPa |
| Deflection at test bearing | 56 µm | – |
| Slope at test bearing | 7.1′ | The floating housing follows it (stud-hole clearance allows 41′); outer/inner ring misalignment ≈ 0 |
| First bending critical speed | ≈ 36,500 rpm | 24× above 1,500 rpm |
| Unbalance force | 0.5 N (1 g) to 1.5 N (3 g) at R20 and 1,500 rpm | Small vs bearing and spring loads |

### 2.3 Captive load mechanism
- **Arrangement:** two identical compression springs (k ≈ 5 N/mm each, individually calibrated with 2 kg and 5 kg masses) on M5 studs. The studs are fixed in the base with a lock-nut and run through Ø5.6 holes in the test housing ears.
  - Springs sit on PTFE washers on the housing top and are compressed by washer + nut + lock-nut.
  - **Settings:** 20 N needs 1.8 mm compression per spring; 50 N needs 4.8 mm, each including the housing's own 2.3 N weight.
  - Set by measuring washer height above the housing with a vernier on both studs, equal within 0.1 mm.
  - The springs pull the housing down, so the **load zone is the top of the bearing**, directly under the sensor.
- **Why it is captive:**
  - Nothing hangs or swings (no dead-weight pendulum adding low-frequency dynamics).
  - Nuts are locked.
  - A **hard-stop block 1.0 mm below the housing** catches it if the bearing or shaft fails.
  - Springs cannot leave the studs.
- **Housing movement:**
  - Free vertically (load direction) and in small tilt (follows shaft slope).
  - Anti-rotation by the two studs.
  - Axial position set by the bearing (inner ring clamped between two collars, outer ring clamped by cover plates).
- **Rejected:** the dead-weight hanger from the review. It is not captive, and it is a pendulum.

### 2.4 Alignment and repeatability
- **Line-bored housings:** all three housings are bored in **one machine setup** (supports on their base faces, test housing on a 15.00 mm shim), so bore centre heights match within ±0.02 mm.
- **Motor centre:** set by the machined pilot bore, with a 0.05/0.1 mm shim pack under the motor plate.
- **Coupling alignment** (dial indicator across the hubs): parallel ≤ 0.05 mm, angular ≤ 0.1°.
- **Disc run-out:** ≤ 0.03 mm TIR.
- **Recorded every session:** shim stacks, collar and fastener torques, spring heights, bearing serial number. Misalignment can later be introduced *deliberately* with these shims, at measured values.

### 2.5 Guarding
- **Guard:** 3 mm polycarbonate enclosure, 232 × ~125 × 112 mm, on aluminium angles screwed to the base. It covers the coupling, shaft, disc, test housing and studs.
  - Lift-off lid with captive screws; cable grommet on the sensor side.
  - The motor's **rear shaft gets a cap guard** (the motor is dual-shaft).
- **Electrical:** latching DC-rated E-stop and T2A fuse in the motor loop, supply current limit 2 A.
- **Isolation rule:** supply OFF + E-stop pressed → shaft stopped → lid off → adjust → lid on → power.

### 2.6 Fabrication requirements (college workshop)
- **Machines:** lathe (disc, pilot bore), vertical mill with boring head or a Ø22 H7 reamer (line-boring the 3 housings), drill and taps M3/M4/M5, bandsaw.
- **Material:** Al 6061-T6 flat bar (≈ 25 × 100 × 300) and a 12 mm plate 330 × 160.

|---|---|---|
| P1 Support housing (60×20×60) | 2 | Bore 22 H7; centre height 40.00 ±0.02; base face flat 0.01; M5 / M3 tapped |

## Check 3 – IIS3DWB → ESP32 data acquisition

### 3.1 Sensor configuration
- **Single-axis mode** (XL_AXIS_SEL).
  - The adapter is bonded to the sensor cube so the sensor's **X axis is vertical**: the load direction, at the load zone.
  - X/Y single-axis noise is 60 µg/√Hz, lower than Z (80).
- **ODR 26.667 kHz; full scale ±8 g (0.244 mg/LSB).**
  - The LSB is ≈ 20× below the ≈ 4.8 mg rms broadband noise, so there is no resolution loss.
  - Headroom against impacts; a clipping counter is reported.
- **Filter:** LPF1 only. Bandwidth 6.3 kHz; the sensor resonance (6.9–7 kHz) lies above the analysis band (1.5–6 kHz).
- **SPI:** **8 MHz**, mode 3, VSPI on the existing pins (CS5 / SCK18 / MISO19 / MOSI23; the sensor allows 10 MHz).
  - Cable ≤ 15 cm, shielded/twisted with interleaved GND.
  - 33 Ω series resistors on SCK and MOSI; 100 nF + 10 µF decoupling at the adapter.
- **FIFO (worst-case sizing):**
  - 7-byte words (tag + 6 data), so 3 KB holds 438 words = 16.4 ms.
  - Watermark **256 words (9.6 ms)**. A burst read takes 1.8 ms at 8 MHz, leaving a **6.8 ms service deadline** before overrun.
  - SPI bus load 19 %.
  - If single-axis mode packs several samples per word (to be confirmed in S2), the margins only improve.
- **Timestamps:** optional timestamp batching in the FIFO (12.5 µs LSB, DEC_TS_BATCH = 32) gives an independent check of sample spacing.

### 3.2 Throughput and transport
- **Frame format: RIG3**, 100 ms frames.

| Element | Size |
|---|---|
| "RIG3" magic | 4 B |
| Header | 76 B (RIG2 fields plus nominal rates, sensor ID, tach edge count, FIFO-overrun / tag-error / clip / UART-drop counters) |
| Vibration | 2,667 × i16 |
| Current | 500 × u16 |
| Tach edge timestamps | ≤ 3 × u32 |
| Checksum | **CRC-32** (replaces RIG2's 16-bit additive sum, which misses many error patterns) |

- **Payload:** **6,429 B per frame = 64.3 kB/s.**
- **921,600 baud 8N1 = 92.2 kB/s, so link utilisation is 70 %.** Selected: it is within the CP2102 limit (1 Mbaud) and needs no hardware change.
  - Three axes at full rate would need 170 kB/s, which is why single-axis mode is required.
- **Fallbacks, used only if test T1 fails:**
  1. A CP2102N board at 2 Mbaud (32 % utilisation).
  2. FIR-decimate by 2 on the ESP32 to 13.33 kHz. The anti-alias filter must suppress the 6.9 kHz sensor resonance; validated against full-rate data.
- **ESP32 buffering:**

| Buffer | Size |
|---|---|
| Vibration ring | 0.5 s = 26 KiB |
| Current ring | 0.5 s = 4.9 KiB |
| UART TX buffer | 16 KiB (≥ 2 frames) |

- **Task layout:** FIFO-read task pinned to core 1 at high priority, woken by the watermark interrupt. ADC DMA and UART run on core 0.
- **Host:** dedicated serial reader thread with a 4 MB buffer; parser verifies CRC-32 and sequence before anything else.
- **Compatibility:** the parser keeps RIG2 (ADXL345) support; rig config gets `fs_vib = 26667`, `vib_band = (1500, 6000)`.

### 3.3 Loss and corruption detection (all counted, never hidden)
- **Frame level:**
  - CRC-32 failure → frame discarded and counted.
  - Sequence-number gap → counted.
- **Sample level:**
  - Absolute first-sample index per stream in every frame. Any discontinuity is an exact gap, filled with NaN and counted.
- **Device level:**
  - IIS3DWB **FIFO_OVR_IA** (latched variant) → overrun counter.
  - FIFO **tag check:** every word must carry the accelerometer tag. A wrong tag means SPI corruption or misalignment → counter, then FIFO flush.
  - WHO_AM_I re-read once per second.
- **Firmware level:** ring-buffer drops, ADC pool overflow, wrong-channel ADC samples, UART TX overflow.

### 3.4 Timing and acquisition acceptance tests (stage S2, bench, before mounting)
| Test | Method | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| T1 Soak | 30 min continuous at full rate | 0 CRC errors, 0 sequence gaps, 0 sample-index gaps, 0 FIFO overruns, 0 tag errors, 0 ring/UART drops |
| T2 Rate | Host clock fit (index vs ESP32 µs) | Vibration rate within ±2 % of 26,667 Hz (datasheet ODR tolerance) and drift < 0.1 % over 30 min; current within ±0.5 % of 5 kHz |
| T3 Jitter | Residuals of the clock fit | Max residual < one watermark period (9.6 ms); no trend |
| T4 Latency | Host receive time − frame time | ≤ 300 ms (live dashboard) |
| T5 Noise floor | Sensor still on foam, motor off | Noise density 100 Hz–6 kHz within 2× of 60 µg/√Hz |
| T6 Cross-sensor | IIS3DWB and ADXL345 on the same cube, tap and ambient excitation | Coherence > 0.9 and amplitude within ±10 % in 50–1,500 Hz (verifies scale, axis and alignment) |
| T7 Stream sync | Motor start step: current rise vs vibration onset, 5 starts | Delay spread ≤ 2 ms across starts |
| T8 Range | Highest condition with the largest unbalance | 0 clipped samples |

## 4. Current sensing (unchanged decision)
- The ACS712-05B stays.
- Gate S5 measures:
  - its noise floor (motor off, CC on);
  - the commutation-ripple line;
  - the current response to a radial-load step (20 → 50 N) and an unbalance step.
- An upgrade (ACS724 #4040, isolated) is bought **only if** the drag/load signals are within 3× of the measured noise floor.
- A non-isolated shunt + INA240 is not used, because it would remove the galvanic isolation between the motor loop and the laptop.

## 5. Fault plan without manual bearing damage
- **Allowed in rev A:** only faults that are **controlled, measurable and reversible**:
  - unbalance (calibrated masses on the disc);
  - parallel/angular misalignment (measured shims);
  - radial-load steps;
  - **physical sensor faults** (loose sensor screw at a recorded torque, intermittent cable, disconnect) for OB2.
- **Bearing defects:** only if a *validated* source of damaged bearings is found (e.g. a documented EDM or laboratory supplier with defect dimensions). That is a separate future decision.
- **Data separation:** rig data trains and tests rig models only. Benchmark models and results stay frozen and are never tuned on rig data, and the reverse.
